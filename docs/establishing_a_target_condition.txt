Establishing a Target Condition
(an original summary of the idea, not a reproduction of any book - see attribution at the end)

WHAT A TARGET CONDITION ACTUALLY IS

A target condition describes a state you want to be true - a pattern of operating, checkable by
looking at it - not a number you want to hit. That distinction is the whole idea, and it's easy
to get backwards.

Say the goal is "sell more of something." The tempting way to write that down is as a target: a
count, a deadline, a quota - "20 sales by Friday." That's an outcome. It tells you what to hope
for. It does not tell you what has to be TRUE, day to day, for that outcome to become reachable.

A target condition instead names the operating state: "a repeatable, checkable, daily practice
exists for putting this in front of a real stranger, regardless of which channel it takes." That's
not a number. It's a description of a process running the way you want it to run. You can look at
any given day and ask "does that condition exist right now, or not" - which you cannot honestly do
with a target like "20 sales," because a target only resolves at the finish line, and by then it's
too late to have learned anything along the way.

WHY THE DIFFERENCE MATTERS

An outcome target can be hit by luck, by gaming it, or by a single insincere transaction, and none
of that tells you anything real about whether the underlying capability exists. If the target is
"one book sold," having a family member buy a copy as a gift satisfies it completely while proving
nothing. A target condition built around a real, repeatable, verifiable practice can't be gamed
the same way - the whole point is the pattern holding up under real conditions, not one lucky
instance clearing a bar.

There's also a trap worth naming directly: it's tempting to write the SOLUTION into the target
condition - "post daily on Reddit" instead of "a repeatable practice exists." Don't. The moment a
specific channel or method gets baked into the target condition, you've quietly turned it into a
plan, and plans are exactly the thing that shouldn't survive first contact with reality unchanged.
Leave the "how" out. That's what Test items are for - the countermeasures get worked out once
you're actually moving, not decided in advance and then defended against evidence.

HOW MUCH TO SPECIFY, AND HOW FAR OUT

It's normal, even correct, not to know exactly how a target condition will be reached at the
moment it's set. If the path were already obvious, there'd be nothing to learn by pursuing it -
you'd just be executing a known plan, not running a real experiment. Being unsure how you'll get
there is a sign the target condition is pitched right, not a gap to panic-fill before starting.

Keep the time horizon short, especially early on - days, not months. A target condition that
reaches too far into the future needs a real plan to bridge the gap, which reintroduces the same
problem: a long plan predicts too much and adapts too little. A short one gets tested, checked, and
revised fast, which is the entire mechanism that makes this useful instead of just aspirational.
